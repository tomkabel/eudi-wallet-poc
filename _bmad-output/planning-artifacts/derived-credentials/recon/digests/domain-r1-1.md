### Q1 European Business Wallets (EBW)

- The Commission proposal for a Regulation on the establishment of European Business Wallets is COM(2025) 838 final (CELEX 52025PC0838), dated "Brussels, 19.11.2025". | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52025PC0838 | EUR-Lex / European Commission | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- The Commission library page also gives the publication date as 19 November 2025 (second source for the date). | https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-regulation-establishment-european-business-wallets | European Commission DG CNECT | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- Procedure 2025/0358(COD). Lead committee is ITRE, rapporteur Eero Heinäluoma (S&D); IMCO and JURI give opinions. Committee vote and decision to open negotiations on 10/09/2026; report A10-0240/2026 tabled 23/09/2026; decision to enter interinstitutional negotiations announced/confirmed in plenary (Rule 72) from 05/10/2026. Stage on OEIL: "Awaiting Parliament's position in 1st reading", meaning trilogues on the committee mandate with no first-reading position yet. | https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2025/0358(COD) | European Parliament Legislative Observatory | live page | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- The Council adopted its general approach on 9 June 2026 and aims for political agreement by the end of 2026. In its position the Council "specif[ies] that the authorisation system established in the proposal is not intended to affect any power of attorney or legal mandates provided under national or Union law". | https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/09/european-business-wallets-council-adopts-negotiating-position/ | Council of the EU | 2026-06-09 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- In the proposal, mandates are a business-wallet construct. Art. 3(1)(c) says an EBW serves "to enable the creation, management and delegation of mandates to authorised representatives". Art. 3(18) defines an "authorised representative" as a natural or legal person acting for the EBW owner "on the basis of an authorisation granted by" that owner. Art. 3(19) defines a "mandate" as the owner's authorisation enabling the representative "to act on behalf of the owner in executing and operating functions of a designated European Business Wallet". So the company (wallet owner) grants the mandate inside its EBW, and the mandate is scoped to operating that wallet. It is not a general power of attorney issued into a person's EUDI Wallet. | EUR-Lex COM(2025) 838, Art. 3 | European Commission | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- The bridge to the EUDI Wallet: Art. 5(1)(c) requires EBWs to request and share owner identification data and EAAs "between European Business Wallets and European Digital Identity Wallets". Art. 5(1)(f) requires EBWs to "issue electronic attestations of attributes to European Business Wallets and European Digital Identity Wallets". Art. 5(1)(h) covers using qualified and non-qualified EAAs so that owners "and their authorised representatives" can authenticate. Art. 6(e) requires remote onboarding of the owner "via an authorised representative with an electronic identification means" at LoA substantial or high. The proposal therefore lets a company's EBW issue an EAA (such as a mandate) into a natural person's EUDI Wallet, but the issuer is the business wallet, not a state registry. | EUR-Lex COM(2025) 838, Arts. 5, 6 | European Commission | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- Business registers: Art. 8 governs "EBW owner identification data". It is issued as a QEAA (by a QTSP), as an EAA "issued by or on behalf of a public sector body responsible for an authentic source" (the PuB-EAA route), or by the Commission for Union entities. Member States notify the authentic sources (Art. 8(2)). Minimum attributes are official name "as recorded in the relevant register" and unique identifier (Art. 8(5)). Art. 9 makes the EUID under Directive (EU) 2017/1132 (the BRIS identifier) the unique identifier, with a fallback identifier where no EUID exists. Art. 3(37) defines "national register". The registry-backed attestation in the proposal is company identity, not "person X may represent company Y". | EUR-Lex COM(2025) 838, Arts. 3(37), 8, 9 | European Commission | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- Art. 3(22) defines "user" with the eIDAS wording that includes "a natural person representing another natural person or a legal person". | EUR-Lex COM(2025) 838, Art. 3(22) | European Commission | 2025-11-19 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory

### Q2 ARF

- The current ARF release is v3.0.0, marked "Latest" and published 2026-07-23. Earlier releases: v2.9.0 on 2026-05-21 and v2.8.0 on 2026-02-02. Later activity is a "Topic AA rr paper v0.8" pre-release on 2026-10-14. | https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/releases/tag/v3.0.0 | European Commission (EUDI Wallet ARF team) | 2026-07-23 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | plan
- ARF main §3 now states that "the topic of Wallet Units for legal persons, possibly containing a legal-person PID, has been removed from this ARF in view of the development of a separate business wallet". Legal-person representation has moved to the EBW track. | https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/blob/main/docs/main/03-roles-within-the-eudi-wallet-ecosystem.md (line ~59) | EC ARF | main branch at v3.0.0+ | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | scope
- Discussion Paper Topic I, "Natural person representing another natural person", explicitly excludes "the case of a natural person representing a legal person". It cites eIDAS Art. 3(1), (3), (4), (5a), Art. 5a(5)(f), Art. 11a(3)(c) and Annex VI item 9 "Powers and mandates to represent natural or legal persons". It sets out that representation attestations SHALL be a distinct attestation type, SHALL state the nature and scope of the representation without the possibility of concealing it, and SHALL be short-lived or revocable. The issuer may draw on national authentic sources (parent–child), or the principal may authorise explicitly (power of attorney). The discussion-topics README lists Topic I as being in a "Refinement Round" with every column "Open", so the topic is still under work. | https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/blob/main/docs/discussion-topics/i-natural-person-representing-another-natural-person.md | EC ARF | undated paper; README live | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | plan
- Integrated text: ARF main §2.6.5 describes "representation attestations". Annex 2 Topic 29 "Representation paradigm" has RP_01: "Attestation Scheme Providers MAY create one or multiple Attestation Rulebooks for representation attestations issued to a natural person representing another natural person... SHALL specify the unique attestation type... validity period, the nature of the representation, and the operations the representative is authorised to perform". RP_02 requires the attestations to be short-lived or revocable by all entities entitled to revoke them under law. The Relying Party "is always aware that it is interacting with a legal representative". | https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/blob/main/docs/annexes/annex-2/annex-2.02-high-level-requirements-by-topic.md (§A.2.3.18) and docs/main/02-eudi-wallet-functionalities.md §2.6.5 | EC ARF | v3.0.0 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory
- The ARF has no rulebook, topic or HLR for natural-person-to-legal-person mandates. The only representation requirements are the person-to-person RP_01/RP_02. | same as above (grep of docs/main and Annex 2) | EC ARF | v3.0.0 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (absence finding) | scope
- Embedded disclosure policies apply to non-qualified EAAs. Annex 2 Topic 43 EDP_01: "A Wallet Unit SHALL enable an Attestation Provider to optionally express an embedded disclosure policy for a QEAA, PuB-EAA, or non-qualified EAA", with a note that eIDAS does not require EDPs for PIDs. EDP_02 and EDP_03 implement the Annex III policies of IR 2024/2979 ("Authorised relying parties only" and "Specific root of trust") per ETSI TS 119 472-3 and evaluate them against the RP registration certificate. Caveat: ARF main §6.6.2.8.1 still says the Regulation requires EDPs "only for QEAAs and PuB-EAAs". Extending them to non-qualified EAAs is therefore an ARF choice beyond the legal minimum, made through Topic D. | Annex 2 §A.2.3.25 (link above); https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/blob/main/docs/discussion-topics/d-embedded-disclosure-policies.md | EC ARF | v3.0.0 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | regulatory

### Q3 Estonia

- Pääsuke's scope is access authorisations to e-services. RIA: "Pääsukese ... abil saab ettevõtte ja asutuse esindaja hallata juurdepääsuvolitusi ühes keskses kohas. Infosüsteem võimaldab hallata selliseid volitusi, mille alusel tekivad juurdepääsud erinevatesse ametiasutustesse ja klienditeeninduskeskkondadesse" (the company or institution representative manages access authorisations that grant access to agencies' customer-service environments). | https://www.ria.ee/riigi-infosusteem/personaalriik/keskne-volituste-haldamise-infosusteem-paasuke | RIA | live page | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | scope
- The Pääsuke general terms (2024) describe two services: (1) authorisation management and (2) an "oraakel" (oracle) that lets connected client applications ("klientrakendus") query a person's valid authorisations. Users see the authorisations they have given and received "üle terve Eesti riigi erinevate infosüsteemide jaoks". The model is an online query by integrated state information systems. Nothing in the terms is a portable signed credential. | https://www.ria.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2024-03/Paasuke-uldtingimused-2024.pdf | RIA | 2024-03 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | scope
- In RIA's EUDI wallet ("digikukkur") Phase III analysis collection (dated 8.04.2026, published 2026-05), chapter 12 "EAA types" analyses QEAA, PuB-EAA and non-qualified EAA in general terms with Estonian examples. A text search turned up no reference to Pääsuke, the Business Register (äriregister), representation rights (esindusõigus) or mandates as planned wallet attestations. Only generic "volitus" wording appears, about signing approvals and device migration. | https://www.ria.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2026-05/Digikukru-III-etapi-analuus.pdf | RIA | 2026-04-08 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (absence in a scraped PDF) | plan
- The RIA digikukkur page (ET) links that analysis and an activity table "Tegevuste tabel EUDIW väljaandmiseks". The page excerpts retrieved showed no mandate or representation attestation plan and no dated timeline for them. | https://www.ria.ee/riigi-infosusteem/elektroonilise-identiteedi-teenused/digikukkur-eudi-wallet | RIA | live page | accessed 2026-11-06 | med | plan
