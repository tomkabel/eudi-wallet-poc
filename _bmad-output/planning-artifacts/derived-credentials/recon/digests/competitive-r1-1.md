# Competitive recon r1-1: derived credentials after PID verification

Decision served: should the wallet PoC add a demo story "verify PID, then issue a derived attestation carrying a fact the PID cannot (mandate/role)", or park it? It also tests the Dock Labs thesis that businesses should issue reusable credentials and get paid when others verify them.

Research firewall: only web sources retrieved on 2026-11-06. No local project files were read.

## Findings

### Q1: Who ships "verify PID, then issue a derived credential"

- Combining a presentation with issuance is a known open problem in OpenID4VCI 1.0. Issue #730 says "a common scenario is the issuance of a credential to a user who authenticates by presenting another credential to the issuer". It also says the Interactive Authorization Endpoint (IAE) that does this cleanly exists only in the 1.1 draft. Implementations on 1.0 use variants that "may not be compatible" and offer different binding security. | https://github.com/openid/OpenID4VCI/issues/730 | OpenID Foundation DCP WG (GitHub) | 2026-04-15 (open) | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | capability
- OpenID4VCI 1.0 itself includes an example flow: "After presenting an ID document, the End-User is prompted to scan a QR code using the Wallet…". | https://openid.net/specs/openid-4-verifiable-credential-issuance-1_0.html | OpenID Foundation | 2025-09-16 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (search snippet only) | capability
- Credo (OWF credo-ts; that it is Animo's stack behind Paradym is an unverified belief) ships "presentation during issuance" in its issuer and holder. The code has `OpenId4VciAuthorizationFlow.PresentationDuringIssuance`, an Authorization Challenge endpoint, `presentationDuringIssuanceSession`, and a CHANGELOG entry: "`presentation` - authorization is completed using an OpenID4VP presentation during issuance (requires `getVerificationSession`)". | https://github.com/openwallet-foundation/credo-ts (packages/openid4vc/CHANGELOG.md, src/openid4vc-issuer/router/authorizationChallengeEndpoint.ts) | OpenWallet Foundation | release date not retrieved | accessed 2026-11-06 | high (code) | capability
- The OWF identity-common-ts (`@openid4vc/oauth2`) changelog says: "add support for the new Interactive Authorization Endpoint from OpenID4VCI 1.1 draft to allow presentation during issuance… experimental". The mdoc package adds a SessionTranscript "for OpenID4VP with Interactive Authorization (OpenID4VCI presentation during issuance)". | https://github.com/openwallet-foundation-labs/identity-common-ts | OWF Labs | date not retrieved | accessed 2026-11-06 | high (code) | capability
- The Paradym product docs have "Authorization servers" and "Attribute providers" pages plus PID issue and verify templates. A grep of those pages found no product-level "presentation during issuance" text, so Paradym's product exposure of the Credo feature is unverified. | https://docs.paradym.id/api-and-dashboard/authorization-servers | Animo | undated | accessed 2026-11-06 | low | capability
- walt.id Issuer2's authorization-code flow sends the user to an external OAuth/OIDC provider (Keycloak, Entra, Auth0). - Truvera (Dock Labs) supports OID4VCI issuance (auth-code with "authProvider"/"claimMap", or pre-authorized) and OID4VP verification as separate API steps. - Dock markets "Delegated Authority: prove a person's role, permissions, and organizational affiliation" and "Reusable Business Verification". This is marketing only and was not checked against docs. | https://www.dock.io/paid-credential-verification (navigation) | Dock Labs | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-06 | low | capability
- Signicat's eID and Wallet Hub "EUDI Wallet connector" sandbox is documented for verification and authentication with a PID (test PID from the iGrant wallet). - Procivis One's docs list an Issuer "for issuing PIDs, QEAAs, PuB-EAAs, and EAAs into any EUDI Wallet". A map of the docs found presentation-submit APIs but no page on PID-gated issuance. | https://docs.procivis.ch/eidas | Procivis | undated | accessed 2026-11-06 | low | capability

### Q2: EU large-scale pilots

- EWC RFC005 "Issue LPID" v1.0 (Approved) applies four checks: (1) the natural person is authenticated at LoA High ("Not done during pilot as there is no LoA High available yet"), (2) the provider verifies their representative rights, (3) an optional signature, and (4) the legal person's status in an authentic source such as a business register. Issuance goes through the PID provider's eService, then an OID4VCI offer. It does not use a PID presentation. | https://github.com/EWC-consortium/eudi-wallet-rfcs/blob/main/ewc-rfc005-issue-legal-person-identification-data.md | EWC consortium | v1.0, date not retrieved | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | pilot
- EWC Rulebook RB004 "Signatory Rights" defines a mandate/role attestation that business registers issue. It "can be issued with any authentification or substancial identity verification", to either an organisational wallet or a natural-person wallet. A natural-person copy lists only the requesting signatory. Complex chained signatories were out of scope "for revocation and data chaining reasons". Verification is left to the relying party, and "modifications… are expected". Attributes: legal_person_name, legal_person_id (EUID), full_name, date_of_birth, signatory_rule (alone or jointly), post.name. | https://github.com/EWC-consortium/eudi-wallet-rulebooks-and-schemas/blob/main/rulebooks/rb_004_signatory_rights.md | EWC consortium | date not retrieved | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | pilot
- The EWC trust list defines service types "Legal Person PID Provider" (LPID) and "Legal Person Wallet Provider" (LPWP). | https://github.com/EWC-consortium/ewc-trust-list | EWC | current | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | pilot
- The cross-pilot synthesis says sustainable business models "remain unclear for all stakeholders". In its words: "who pays for wallet operations, credential issuance, verification services, and authentication… have not been satisfactorily answered". It also finds that "absence of harmonised legal frameworks for representation, including parental authority, guardianship, and business mandates, creates particular challenges". | https://media.meeco.me/public-assets/reports/Meeco_Report_EUDI_Large_Scale_Pilots.pdf | Meeco (secondary synthesis of LSP final reports) | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (secondary) | pilot
- POTENTIAL (per Meeco): bank accounts opened with wallet KYC, and SIM registration with PID plus MSISDN issuance. Its recommendations include "Enable reuse of financial attestations (IBAN, tax ID, MSISDN) across institutions" and harmonising AML so wallet attestations are accepted. | same Meeco PDF | Meeco | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med | pilot
- NOBID (per Meeco): a Norwegian PID was used to obtain Italian fiscal codes, which is PID-based issuance of a derived attestation. It also tested parental representation. Lessons: "Legal complexity in representation", "Missing business models", and a recommendation to "Define sustainable business models – clarify who pays for authentication, issuance, and verification". | same Meeco PDF | Meeco | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med | pilot
- EWC (per Meeco): travel and payment use cases (ferry, museum, airline, hotel). One lesson: "Business models must work for all public and private participants." | same Meeco PDF | Meeco | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med | pilot
- WE BUILD is the successor LSP for business, payments and legal representation, and covers PID/LPID. It held its 2026 general assembly in Bucharest. | https://www.webuildconsortium.eu/news/simplifying-cross-border-business-we-build-consortium-meets-in-bucharest-to-take-stock-of-its-first-year-and-plan-the-road-ahead | WE BUILD | ~2026-11-04 | accessed 2026-11-06 | low (snippet) | pilot

### Q3: Paid verifier demand in Estonia and the Nordics

### Q4: Paying per verification without the issuer learning where the credential is used

- Dock/Truvera mechanism (from the docs): KVAC (Keyed Verification Anonymous Credentials) "lock" the credential. "During the verification process, the issuer is asked to provide an additional secret… in order to unlock it." The verifier sends the issuer "2 random looking data items (A, B)" and the issuer checks "A equals B * issuer-secret-key". A is the holder's MAC, randomised. **The issuer is not blind to the verifier**: "The Issuer sees that a credential based on a specific schema was shared with a specific Verifier, but learns nothing about the Holder." Holder privacy depends on herd size per schema. The administrator sets a per-schema price and Truvera takes "a small percentage fee". | https://docs.truvera.io/truvera-workspace/monetizing-credentials.md | Dock Labs | undated (docs current) | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | mechanism
- The Dock marketing page agrees: the "ecosystem administrator sees that an ID credential from a specific issuer and based on a specific schema was shared with a verifier". Verification needs ecosystem membership, and KVAC "ensures that credentials can only be verified by participants in your ecosystem". This is a closed ecosystem, not open EUDI verification. | https://www.dock.io/paid-credential-verification | Dock Labs | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | mechanism
- Truvera docs say: "When verifying ecosystem bound credentials with paid schemas, the issuance date can not be revealed". | https://docs.truvera.io/truvera-workspace/verify-credentials | Dock Labs | 2026-10-04 | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (snippet) | mechanism
- cheqd mechanism: in "Verifier pays Issuer" the verifier pays to decrypt an encrypted Bitstring Status List (a DID-Linked Resource gated by payment conditions) on the cheqd network. Each status list has one flat price, so payments are not correlatable to a holder ("herd privacy"; minimum 131,072 entries). "Transactions will be visible on the network." The payment goes to the issuer per status list, so the docs do not claim that the issuer is blind to which verifier paid. | https://docs.cheqd.io/product/studio/payments/learn/privacy-considerations | cheqd | undated | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | mechanism
- cheqd's worked example: the verifier pays "$20.00 to Bright University" to verify a diploma's status. | https://docs.cheqd.io/product/learning-docs/cheq/credential-payments/verifier-pays-issuer | cheqd | "last updated 2 years ago" | accessed 2026-11-06 | high | mechanism
- **No documented design was found that keeps the issuer blind to the verifier while still paying per verification.** Dock explicitly reveals the verifier to the issuer. cheqd hides the holder but not the payer. | — | — | — | accessed 2026-11-06 | med (absence) | mechanism

## Leads

- OpenID4VCI 1.1 draft, Interactive Authorization Endpoint: the standard path for "present PID, then get the attestation". Track its status and issue #730 for 1.0 workarounds (variant 1, separated flows; variant 2, OID4VP nested in the authorization step).
- Credo `presentation` authorization mode plus `getVerificationSession`: the only shipping open-source issuer found that gates OID4VCI issuance on an OID4VP presentation. Check the release date and version.
- EWC RB004 Signatory Rights attestation: a ready-made attribute schema for a "mandate/role" derived attestation. The issuer is a business register, not a private firm.
- EWC final report (https://eudiwalletconsortium.org…), POTENTIAL final report (https://digital-identity-wallet.eu) and NOBID technical reports (https://www.nobidconsortium.com/NOBID): read the primaries rather than the Meeco synthesis.
- The WE BUILD LSP (legal representation, LPID) is the live successor work.
- Interac Verified credential service for business: a bank-derived reusable credential. Pricing was not found.
- The SK price-list figures, which were not extracted.

## Looked for, not found

- Doc-level evidence that Signicat, IDnow, Lissi, Procivis, Truvity, walt.id or Truvera issue an attestation gated on a PID presentation. Only Credo/OWF code was found. IDnow, Lissi and Truvity searches returned only homepages.
- Any PID-presentation authentication option in the EU reference Python issuer README.
- Any relying party in Estonia or the Nordics paying for a business-issued (non-eID) attestation.
- Any documented pay-per-verify scheme in which the issuer cannot see which verifier paid. Dock reveals it outright. cheqd does not claim to hide it.
- A primary Interac statement calling Verified.Me shut down or a failure. Only the rename and the consumer-app retirement are documented.
- EWC, POTENTIAL and NOBID primary deliverables. Only the secondary Meeco synthesis (Jan 2026) was read.
- A Dock "trust fee" design or an EWC trust-fee design. None found.
